Leaders around the world are facing a surprising problem. Companies are spending heavily on AI, yet those investments are barely moving the bottom line. Technology alone no longer sets a business apart. It has become the price of entry.

## The AI value gap

The numbers show how wide the gap is. McKinsey reports that 78% of organizations now use AI in at least one business function. Yet BCG finds that 74% of companies have yet to see meaningful value from it.

Companies are deploying AI, but most are not getting the results they expected. The root cause is less about the technology and more about how people adopt it and put it to work.

## Why AI initiatives stall

AI initiatives rarely stall because of IT failures. They stall because of people. Even with strong infrastructure in place, employees may lack the skills to use AI well. Handing people advanced tools won't raise productivity if they can't apply them to real business problems.

Leaders need to treat AI as a fundamental shift in what their people can do, not as another software upgrade.

## From AI literacy to AI fluency

To turn AI investment into measurable results, organizations have to move beyond basic awareness. The goal is to progress from **AI literacy** to genuine **AI fluency**. That takes more than a one-off training session. It takes a deliberate, structured **Literacy-to-Fluency Framework**:

1. **Literacy: set the baseline.** Every employee understands core AI concepts, the tools available to them, and the risks involved, including data privacy, ethics, security and compliance.
2. **Adoption: bring AI into the flow of work.** Training is built into daily tasks. Employees learn by doing, applying AI to specific projects and pilots to discover what works in their own context.
3. **Fluency: from doing things better to doing better things.** Fluent teams don't just use AI. They reimagine workflows, redesign processes and build solutions that weren't possible before.

## Know your skills before you build them

Talent intelligence is central to skills-based transformation and to getting a return on AI investment. An AI-enabled talent intelligence platform helps an organization see the skills it has, find the gaps that matter, and direct training where it will have the most impact.

Consider a global manufacturer rolling out AI across engineering and operations. Instead of sending everyone through the same generic course, it maps its workforce's current skills against the capabilities its AI roadmap requires. Within weeks, leaders can see exactly where the critical gaps are. They focus training budget on those gaps, move people with adjacent skills into new AI-related roles, and hire externally only where internal talent can't fill the need. As business priorities shift, the skills picture updates with them, keeping the right people in the right seats.

## Skills, not job titles

Static job titles slow organizations down. They create silos and limit how quickly talent can move to where it's needed. More organizations are piloting skills-based models that deploy people based on what they can do rather than the fixed role they hold. This approach breaks down barriers between teams and builds a more agile, responsive workforce.

## The bottom line

The organizations that get the most from AI will not be the ones with the most advanced tools. They will be the ones whose people are ready, skilled and confident enough to use them.
