**S2V (Skills-Signal-to-Value) shows which skills move which business outcomes, using evidence from completed work.** At its core is the Skill Graph: one governed map that connects every skill to the tasks that need it, the jobs those tasks make up, and the KPIs and outcomes they move.

## The problem: skills without context

A skill on its own has no address. "Negotiation, level 3" says nothing about which work needs it, which job depends on it, or which outcome suffers when it is missing.

Most organizations hold four separate lists, each owned by a different team: skills in a learning library, jobs in an HR architecture, tasks scattered across CRM and delivery tools, and outcomes in OKRs and KPIs. Every hard question a leader asks crosses at least three of them. So each answer is rebuilt by hand in a spreadsheet, and skills data is mostly self-declared and out of date.

## The idea: one graph, from skill to outcome

![The S2V Skill Graph: a skill enables a task, the task moves a KPI, and the KPI indicates progress on an outcome. People hold jobs made of tasks, and evidence from their work proves skills.](images/s2v-skill-graph.svg)

The spine of the graph is a value path. A **skill** enables a **task**, the task moves a **KPI**, and the KPI shows progress on an **outcome**. **Jobs** are bundles of tasks, and **evidence** from real work, such as a closed deal or a well-scored customer call, shows which **people** have proven which skills.

Every link carries a weight and a named owner. AI can propose links, but only an owner can publish them, so the graph stays a governed, explainable model rather than a black box.

## Four questions the graph answers

1. **Why does this skill matter?** Follow the path from the skill to the outcome, and see how much the outcome depends on it.
2. **Can this team deliver this outcome?** Compare what the outcome's tasks require with what people have proven, and rank the gaps by how much they put the outcome at risk.
3. **How do we know?** Every score opens to the evidence behind it: the task, the source record and who validated it.
4. **What if the work changes?** When AI takes over part of a task, see which skills lose or gain demand and which KPIs are exposed.

## Evidence, not self-assessment

S2V reads evidence from the systems where work already happens: CRM, call recording, documents and learning platforms. Rules handle structured events. AI reads unstructured work, such as call transcripts and proposals, and must quote the source for every skill it proposes. A manager reviews AI suggestions before they count toward any staffing decision.

Skill value is treated as a hypothesis, not a fact. Each quarter, S2V compares the value the graph predicts with what the data shows, and the owners adjust the weights. That loop is how the graph learns.

## Built on trust

- **Development, never judgment.** Skill data supports growth, staffing and learning. It is never used for pay, ratings or termination.
- **Transparent to the individual.** People see every record and AI inference about them, and can dispute it.
- **Minimal data.** Only named work events and approved artefacts are used. No email, chat or activity monitoring.
- **Results for groups.** Links between skills and outcomes are reported for teams, not individuals.

## Where it starts

The first pilot focuses on a sales team, where outcomes are measured weekly and data is clean. One outcome, such as quarterly bookings, is broken into the KPIs that drive it: pipeline, win rate and deal size, each with leading indicators. Every week, leaders review the skills with the highest gap value against that outcome and decide whether to staff, pair, train or accept.

The goal is simple: change real decisions, and trace them to a number the business already cares about.
